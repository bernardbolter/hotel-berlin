Every work, and a page of its own

The index that holds all of it, and the page each mural gets so it can be found on its own terms. The work page is built for a thin record: a photograph, an artist and a location are enough to fill it, because the rest of the page is made of things the site already knows — which floor, what else is on that floor, what else the artist made here.

Decisions in this comp

  • Tiles link, they no longer open in place

    The panel that opened under the row was right when a work was a detail of a page. A work with its own URL is a thing Google and an assistant can point at, which is the whole reason for making these pages. One behaviour, not two: every tile is a link.

  • The floor strip is the page's ballast

    Eleven bars, one per level, the work's floor in teal. It costs an editor nothing, it can never go stale, and it does the thing a guest actually wants: tells them where to go. It also fills the space a second photograph would fill, on the days when there isn't one.

  • The artist list at the bottom of the index

    Names with a count each. It reads as a proper collection rather than a wall of tiles, and every name is an internal link, which is what makes the artists findable as people rather than as captions.

  • Untitled works lead with the artist

    No „Ohne Titel" as a headline. The artist's name takes the h1, and the title simply isn't there — in the page and in the structured data alike.

  • The two shows sit above the grid, not inside it

    Same order as the hub: closing soonest first. When both galleries are between shows the band disappears and the grid moves up.

  • Detail photographs are optional and rare

    Three squares under the main image when they exist. Most murals will have one photograph and a context shot, and the page is designed for exactly that.
