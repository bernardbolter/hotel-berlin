Two galleries, one section, seven states

The FKKB and the photo gallery both run monthly shows, so two current exhibitions is the normal state, not the exception. This draws that state first and then every way it can thin out: one show, one show plus an announced one, none, and no art at all. Nothing here is curated. Every tile is chosen by date and by recency, so the section is right on a morning when nobody has logged in for a month.

Why it's built this way

  • Two monthly shows is the resting state

    So state A is the design, and the others are what happens when it thins. A layout designed for one show and stretched to two would put the second gallery in a small tile next to a mural, which reads as less important than it is. Two equal wide tiles say: two galleries, both current.

  • Ending soonest goes first

    Between two current shows, the one closing in four days is the more useful thing to tell a guest who is here for two nights. It also means the lead tile changes by itself over the month, without anyone touching it.

  • A show leaves on its closing date

    status is worked out from the dates, not set by hand. On the day after a show closes it stops being „Jetzt" everywhere: the mosaic, the art page and the agenda. That is the whole reason exhibitions are one record.

  • An announced show is worth a tile

    „Ab 12.10." with an outlined chip, so it reads as a promise rather than something you can see today. It gives the galleries a reason to tell the hotel their dates early, which is the behaviour we want.

  • Empty degrades to art, not to apology

    With no shows at all, the section is simply the works in the building, led by the pinned or newest one. No „Zur Zeit keine Ausstellung", because that line is a statement that goes stale and makes an ordinary month look like a failure.

  • Tiles are dark because there are no photos

    What you see is the real empty state. A tile with a photograph keeps the same caption block over the image. As you upload, the section fills in by itself, newest first.
