Your "cards flowing down the page", kept. Captions lead with the location, as on the wall. Clicking a work opens a panel under its row (like an image search), with the location, the story and the details, and the address bar follows. The artist is the link out, to their person page, which also connects to the people map. A single work doesn't get its own page; an artist does, because artists recur across works, exhibitions and recommendations.

The Pisa73 artwork text, year and size are invented for the comp.

How the anchors behave

  • Adding or deleting never breaks anything for search

    Search engines index /ausstattung, not /ausstattung#sauna. Items coming and going just change the page. No redirects, no 404s. A stale link to a deleted item lands at the top of the page.

  • The anchor is the slug, locked after creation

    Renaming "Sauna" to "Sauna & Sanarium" keeps #sauna. "Hide" rather than "delete" keeps the slug reserved.

  • Structured data uses the same anchors

    Each item is a JSON-LD node with @id …/ausstattung#sauna, attached to the hotel. It appears and disappears with the item. Pending items and unconfirmed hours are left out.

  • Everything is in the HTML

    Closed rows and closed art panels still contain their text, so crawlers, screen readers and people without JavaScript see all of it. Native <details> for the rows, a real button with aria-expanded for the art panel. Focus moves into the panel and Escape closes it.

  • Mobile

    Amenity rows collapse to icon · name · the first fact, and open to full width. Events: time and title on one line, place and price below. Art: two columns, and the panel opens below the pair.
